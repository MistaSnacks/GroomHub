A full dog grooming appointment in Seattle or Portland typically takes 90 to 180 minutes of active grooming, but your dog may be at the salon for 3 to 4 hours total because of bath queues, drying time, and post-finish kennel waits. A bath-only service runs 30 to 90 minutes. Mobile grooming is faster end-to-end at 60 to 90 minutes because there is no queue.

If you have ever wondered why a "90-minute" full groom turns into a half-day appointment, the math below explains it.

## Quick Answer: Grooming Time by Service Type

| Service | Active grooming time | Typical salon stay |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Bath & brush (small short coat) | 15–45 min | 60–90 min |
| Bath & brush (medium short coat) | 30–75 min | 90–120 min |
| Bath & brush (large double coat) | 60–120 min | 2–3 hours |
| Full groom (small breed, maintained) | 60–90 min | 2–3 hours |
| Full groom (medium breed) | 75–105 min | 2.5–3.5 hours |
| Full groom (large or Doodle) | 105–150 min | 3–4 hours |
| Double-coat deshed (Husky, GSD) | 60–120 min | 2–3 hours |
| Hand stripping (terriers, full strip) | 90–300 min | 2–5 hours |
| Puppy first groom | 30–60 min | 60–90 min |
| Mobile (door-to-door) | 60–90 min | 60–90 min |
| Express nail trim only | 5–20 min | 5–20 min |

The "active grooming time" is what your groomer is hands-on with your dog. The "salon stay" includes the bath queue, drying time, and the wait between the table and pickup.

## Full Groom Time by Breed and Coat Type

This is what most owners actually want to know: how long does a full groom take for *my* dog?

| Breed / coat type | Active groom time |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Maltese, Yorkie, Shih Tzu (small, single coat) | 60–120 min |
| Bichon, Cavalier, Cocker Spaniel | 75–120 min |
| Goldendoodle, Labradoodle (medium) | 90–150 min |
| Goldendoodle (large or matted) | 120–240 min |
| Standard Poodle (scissor finish) | 150–240 min |
| Bernese Mountain Dog | 120–180 min |
| Husky, Samoyed, Malamute (deshed) | 90–150 min |
| Border Terrier (hand strip) | 120–180 min |
| Cairn Terrier (full hand strip, show coat) | up to 300 min |

Coat type matters more than dog size. A 12-pound Shih Tzu in full coat can take longer than a 70-pound Lab, because hand-scissoring small breeds is detail work and Labs only need a bath, blowout, and nail trim.

## Why Your Dog Is at the Salon for 4 Hours

Here is the question I get most often: "You said 90 minutes. Why is my dog there for 4?"

The answer is that active grooming is only one piece of the visit. A typical PNW salon runs one or two bathers, one or two stylists, and a single high-velocity dryer station. Your dog cycles through a queue:

1. Check-in and intake (10 minutes)
2. Pre-brush and dematting (10 to 30 minutes)
3. Wait for bath station (15 to 45 minutes)
4. Bath, conditioner, sanitary trim (20 to 30 minutes)
5. Wait for dryer station (15 to 30 minutes)
6. Hand-dry with high-velocity dryer (20 to 45 minutes)
7. Wait for the table (10 to 30 minutes)
8. Haircut, nails, ears, finishing (45 to 90 minutes)
9. Final brush, sanitation, kennel rest until pickup (10 to 30 minutes)

Add it up and you get 2.5 to 5.5 hours, even though your groomer is only hands-on for 90 to 150 minutes. Your dog is not being neglected during the wait. They are resting in a kennel between stages, which is actually safer than rushing them through wet-to-dry-to-table without breaks.

A typical PNW groomer handles 6 to 8 dogs a day. With multiple dogs sharing one or two bath stations, queue time is unavoidable in a salon model.

<Callout>
Why drying takes so long: a dense Goldendoodle coat or a Husky undercoat needs full hand-drying with a high-velocity dryer to prevent hot spots and matting. Cage drying is faster but riskier. Most PNW salons hand-dry, which is the safer choice and adds 20 to 45 minutes per dog.
</Callout>

## Mobile vs. Salon: Why Mobile Is Faster End-to-End

Mobile grooming feels faster because there is no queue. One van, one dog, one continuous block of time.

A mobile Goldendoodle full groom that takes 90 minutes in the van might take 3 to 4 hours at a salon for the same dog, because the salon is batching multiple dogs through shared stations. The active grooming time is similar. The difference is wait time.

## What Stretches Your Appointment

Six factors can add 30 to 90 minutes to your active groom time:

**Coat condition.** Mats and packed undercoat are the biggest variable. A mildly tangled coat adds 10 to 20 minutes. Severe matting can add 60+ minutes, and in some cases your groomer will recommend a shave-down for your dog's welfare. See our [matted dog grooming guide](/blog/severely-matted-dog-grooming-guide) for what to expect.

**Behavior.** A dog that fights nail trims, panics at the dryer, or wiggles on the table forces the groomer to slow down. About 1 in 4 appointments runs long for behavioral reasons. Puppies and rescue dogs especially benefit from desensitization at home before their appointment.

**Skin issues.** Hot spots, allergies, or active infections require medicated shampoos with longer contact time and gentler hand-drying around affected areas.

**Drying method.** Hand-drying with a high-velocity dryer is the gold standard. It is faster and safer than passive cage drying, but it is labor-intensive and needs the groomer's full attention.

**Salon scheduling style.** Some salons run "block" appointments where one groomer handles your dog start-to-finish. This means a shorter total stay but a tighter window. Other salons run an assembly line where bathing, drying, and styling are split across staff. Assembly lines stretch the visit but let dogs rest between stages.

**Your dog's coat density.** This is the PNW factor. Long, wet winters produce thicker undercoats than dry-climate dogs grow. Spring and fall shedding seasons can add 15 to 30 minutes per appointment in Seattle and Portland salons.

## PNW-Specific Time Factors

A few things stretch grooming time in the Pacific Northwest specifically:

**The wet-coat tax.** A dog walked through PNW rain shows up damp. Most groomers add 10 to 15 minutes for towel pre-dry and a longer high-velocity blowout to prevent matting and hot spots. Schedule appointments after a dry stretch when possible.

**Doodle density.** Seattle and Portland are doodle-heavy markets. A Goldendoodle or Bernedoodle in a PNW winter coat commonly adds 30 to 60 minutes over the same breed's grooming time in a drier climate.

**Shedding-season surge.** Spring (March to May) and fall blow out heavier in the PNW because dogs grow thicker undercoats over long, wet winters. Appointments stretch 15 to 30 minutes during these windows. Book your spring deshed early.

**Booking lead times.** Popular Seattle and Portland mobile groomers book 2 to 4 weeks out in spring. Saturday slots disappear first.

## Tips to Keep Your Appointment Shorter

You cannot speed up the dryer, but you can cut 20 to 60 minutes off your appointment with home prep:

- **Brush twice a week** between appointments. Mat-free coats cut bath and dry time substantially.
- **Wipe paws and belly after rain walks.** Drying a damp dog from scratch takes longer than drying a clean, mostly-dry dog.
- **Schedule a deshed every 8 weeks** for double-coated breeds. Loose undercoat removed at home keeps appointments shorter.
- **Trim sanitary areas** at home if your dog tolerates it. Even basic paw-pad trimming saves the groomer time.
- **Match your appointment cadence to coat type.** Most maintained dogs do best at every 4 to 8 weeks. See [how often you should groom your dog](/blog/how-often-should-you-groom-your-dog) for breed-specific schedules.
- **Ask about express slots** if your dog only needs nails or a quick tidy. Some salons offer 20 to 30-minute walk-in services.

Pro tip: When booking, tell your groomer if you want the shortest possible visit. Some PNW salons offer "in-and-out" express scheduling for small breeds in good coat condition, where you get a 90-minute appointment instead of a 3-hour one. It often costs a small premium.
</Callout>

## Common Misconceptions

**"My dog was there 4 hours. Did they actually groom her the whole time?"** No, and that is normal. Active grooming was likely 90 to 120 minutes. The rest was queue time, hand-drying time that cannot be rushed safely, and post-finish kennel rest until pickup.

**"Bigger dog means more time, always."** Mostly, but not always. A 12-pound Shih Tzu in full coat can take longer than a 70-pound Labrador. Coat type beats size.

**"Mobile takes longer than salon."** Other way around. Mobile is one continuous block with no queue. Salon stays are longer because of bath, dryer, and table queues, even though active grooming time is similar.

**"I can save money by skipping a few appointments."** Skipping appointments means more matting, more dematting time, and surcharges that often cost more than the appointment you skipped. The most expensive groom is the one after a 3-month gap.

## Bottom Line

Plan on 60 to 90 minutes for a bath, 90 to 180 minutes of active time for a full groom, and 2 to 4 hours for the total salon stay. Mobile grooming compresses everything into a continuous 60 to 90 minutes. Coat type, behavior, and matting move the needle more than dog size.
