Mobile dog grooming costs $65 to $180 for a full groom in 2026, depending on your dog's size, breed, and coat condition. That's roughly 15 to 30 percent more than a traditional salon visit. For most dog owners, the premium buys convenience, less stress on your pet, and an hour of your Saturday back.

Here's the full pricing breakdown, what you're actually paying for, and how to decide if mobile grooming is worth it for your dog.

## Mobile Dog Grooming Prices by Size

The biggest factor in mobile grooming cost is your dog's size. Larger dogs take more time, more product, and more physical effort for the groomer.

| Dog Size | Weight | Full Mobile Groom | Bath & Brush Only |
|----------|--------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Small | Under 15 lbs | $65 - $90 | $45 - $60 |
| Medium | 15 - 40 lbs | $85 - $120 | $55 - $75 |
| Large | 40 - 70 lbs | $100 - $150 | $70 - $95 |
| Extra Large | 70+ lbs | $130 - $180+ | $85 - $120 |

These ranges represent national averages. Pacific Northwest prices tend to land in the mid-to-upper portion of these ranges, particularly in Seattle and the Portland metro area. A full mobile groom for a medium-sized Goldendoodle in Seattle will typically run $120 to $160.

<Callout>
If your dog hasn't been groomed in a while and has significant matting, expect to pay more. De-matting surcharges of $20 to $50 are standard, and severe matting may require a full shave-down regardless of the style you wanted.
</Callout>

## Mobile vs. Salon: Price Comparison

Mobile grooming costs more than salon grooming. The question is whether the premium is worth it for your situation.

| Service | Salon Price | Mobile Price | Premium |
|---------|------------|--------------|---------|
| Small dog full groom | $50 - $70 | $65 - $90 | ~25% |
| Medium dog full groom | $70 - $100 | $85 - $120 | ~20% |
| Large dog full groom | $85 - $130 | $100 - $150 | ~15% |
| XL dog full groom | $110 - $150 | $130 - $180+ | ~20% |
| Bath & brush (medium) | $40 - $60 | $55 - $75 | ~25% |
| Nail trim only | $15 - $20 | $25 - $35 | ~50% |

The premium covers real costs. Mobile groomers operate a fully equipped grooming van (think: hot water system, professional bathing station, high-velocity dryer, generator, grooming table) and drive it to your door. Fuel, vehicle maintenance, insurance, and the simple reality that they can only see one dog at a time all factor into that higher price.

For standalone services like nail trims, the premium percentage is higher because the groomer still makes the same trip. Most mobile groomers offer better value on full groom packages than a la carte services.

## What's Included in a Mobile Groom?

A standard full mobile groom typically includes:

- Bath with professional-grade shampoo and conditioner
- Blow-dry with a high-velocity dryer
- Haircut or trim to breed standard (or your preference)
- Nail trimming or grinding
- Ear cleaning
- Sanitary trim
- Paw pad trim
- Light cologne or finishing spray

This is essentially the same service you'd get at a salon. The difference is the setting: your dog gets one-on-one attention in a self-contained van, with no other dogs around, no cage time between steps, and no waiting.

## What Affects Mobile Grooming Prices?

Beyond size, several factors influence what you'll pay.

### Breed and Coat Type

A short-coated Beagle is a 45-minute job. A full-coated Standard Poodle is two hours or more. Breeds with thick double coats (Huskies, Malamutes, Samoyeds) or curly, mat-prone coats (Doodles, Bichons, Cocker Spaniels) cost more because they require significantly more time and effort.

### Coat Condition

If your dog comes to the appointment matted, dirty, or hasn't been groomed in months, the groomer needs more time to safely work through the coat. Regular grooming every 4 to 8 weeks keeps costs predictable. Letting it go for 3+ months almost always results in higher charges.

### Your Location

Urban areas with higher costs of living charge more. In the Pacific Northwest, Seattle metro prices run 10 to 20 percent higher than smaller cities like Olympia, Salem, or Bellingham. Portland falls somewhere in between.

### Frequency and Loyalty

Many mobile groomers offer a discount for recurring appointments (every 4 to 6 weeks). Some offer 10 to 15 percent off when you pre-book your next session. Consistency saves you money and keeps your dog's coat in better shape, which means faster appointments and fewer surcharges.

## Why Mobile Grooming Costs More (And Why People Pay It)

The price premium isn't just about convenience, though convenience is a big part of it. Here's what you're getting for the extra money:

### Less Stress for Your Dog

This is the number one reason people switch to mobile grooming. Dogs who get anxious at the salon, bark at other dogs, or panic in the car don't have to deal with any of that. The groomer parks in your driveway, your dog walks out, gets groomed in a quiet one-on-one environment, and walks back inside. For senior dogs, reactive dogs, or dogs with anxiety issues, this alone can justify the extra cost.

### Faster Turnaround

A salon visit often takes 3 to 4 hours from drop-off to pickup, even though the actual grooming is only 1 to 2 hours. The rest is waiting time. Mobile grooming takes 60 to 90 minutes, start to finish. Your dog is back inside before lunch.

### One-on-One Attention

Your dog is the only animal in the van. No cage drying, no waiting in a kennel, no stress from other dogs barking. The groomer focuses entirely on your pet for the entire session.

### No Transportation Hassle

Loading a 70-pound wet dog into the backseat of your car on a rainy Tuesday in Seattle is nobody's idea of a good time. Mobile grooming removes that step entirely.

## Mobile Grooming in the Pacific Northwest

The PNW climate makes mobile grooming particularly appealing. With 150+ rainy days per year in Seattle and Portland, dogs get dirty faster. That means more frequent grooming, which means more trips to the salon, or you can have the salon come to you.

A few PNW-specific considerations:

- **Mud season runs October through April.** If your dog plays in the yard or goes on trail hikes, you may need grooming every 4 to 5 weeks instead of every 6 to 8 during the wet months. Mobile grooming makes that easier to maintain.
- **Parking matters.** Mobile grooming vans need a driveway or street parking spot with access to a power outlet (some are fully self-contained, but not all). Check with your groomer about parking requirements before booking.
- **Booking lead times are longer.** Popular mobile groomers in Seattle and Portland book out 2 to 4 weeks in advance, especially during spring shedding season (March through May). Plan ahead.

## How to Save Money on Mobile Grooming

Mobile grooming doesn't have to break the bank. Here are practical ways to keep costs down:

1. **Maintain the coat between visits.** Brush your dog 2 to 3 times per week. This prevents matting and keeps appointments shorter, which saves you money. A $15 slicker brush pays for itself after one avoided de-matting surcharge.

2. **Book on a recurring schedule.** Regular appointments (every 4 to 6 weeks) keep your dog's coat in good shape and many groomers offer repeat-client discounts.

3. **Skip the extras when you can.** Not every visit needs teeth brushing and a blueberry facial. Rotate add-ons so you're not paying for the full package every time.

4. **Ask about multi-dog discounts.** If you have two or more dogs, most mobile groomers will offer a discount since they're already making the trip.

5. **Do basic maintenance at home.** Learn to trim nails, clean ears, and do paw pad trims between professional visits. Your groomer can show you how.

## Is Mobile Dog Grooming Worth It?

For many dog owners, yes. The math works out better than it first appears. When you factor in drive time, gas, the hours your dog spends at the salon, and the wear on your car seats, mobile grooming's 15 to 30 percent premium starts to look reasonable.

It's especially worth it if:

- Your dog gets stressed at the salon
- You have a large dog that's difficult to transport
- You have multiple dogs (mobile groomers handle them back-to-back in your driveway)
- You live in an apartment without easy car access
- Your schedule makes drop-off and pickup windows difficult

If your dog is calm at the salon, you're on a tight budget, and you don't mind the drive, a traditional salon is perfectly fine. There's no wrong answer here. The best grooming option is the one that works for you and your dog.
