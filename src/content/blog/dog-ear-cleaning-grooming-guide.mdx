A groomer can clean a dog’s **healthy, non-painful ears** as part of a hygiene service, but a groomer should not diagnose or treat an ear infection. If your dog has pain, redness, swelling, strong odor, discharge, or repeated head shaking, call a veterinarian before booking routine ear cleaning. [Cornell University College of Veterinary Medicine](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/how-clean-your-dogs-ears) and [VCA Animal Hospitals](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/instructions-for-ear-cleaning-in-dogs) both advise stopping when cleaning is painful and getting veterinary guidance for inflamed or painful ears.

## What can a groomer do?

For a dog with healthy ears, a groomer may inspect the visible ear, apply a dog-specific cleaning solution, and wipe away visible debris with cotton or gauze. The service is for routine hygiene, not a substitute for an examination, cytology, prescription medication, or treatment for mites or infection.

Ask the provider exactly what “ear cleaning” means. It may be included with a bath or full groom, offered as a walk-in add-on, or sold as a separate hygiene service. Do not assume that every listing offers ear cleaning, that the service is available without a full groom, or that a groomer can handle a painful ear.

## What happens during a routine cleaning?

A safe routine usually looks like this:

1. **The provider checks whether the ear looks suitable for routine cleaning.** Redness, swelling, odor, discharge, or pain should change the plan.
2. **The provider uses a product intended for dogs.** Ask whether the solution is appropriate for your dog and whether your veterinarian has recommended a particular cleaner.
3. **The ear flap and visible outer canal are wiped gently.** [VCA’s instructions](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/instructions-for-ear-cleaning-in-dogs) say not to insert a bottle tip and not to use cotton-tipped applicators.
4. **Your dog gets a break if handling becomes stressful or painful.** A groomer should explain what they could and could not complete rather than forcing the service.
5. **You receive a handoff.** Ask what the groomer observed and whether they recommend a veterinary appointment.

You may see a dog shake its head after solution is applied. That can be part of moving loosened debris toward the outer opening, but it does not prove that an ear is healthy or that an infection has been treated.

## What should never go in a dog’s ear?

Do not use cotton swabs or push tools down the canal. They can push debris deeper or injure the canal. Avoid household mixtures and do not choose alcohol- or hydrogen-peroxide-based products for an inflamed ear unless your veterinarian specifically directs you. [Cornell’s guidance](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/how-clean-your-dogs-ears) and [Merck Veterinary Manual’s cleaning table](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/multimedia/table/how-to-clean-your-dogs-ears) recommend dog-appropriate cleaning solutions and gentle cotton or gauze wiping.

A groomer should not diagnose yeast, bacteria, ear mites, allergies, or a ruptured eardrum from appearance alone. Those concerns need veterinary assessment.

## When should a vet handle the ears instead?

Contact a veterinarian before cleaning if your dog has any of these signs:

- Pain when the ear is touched
- Marked redness, swelling, or bleeding
- Strong or unusual odor
- New or heavy discharge
- Repeated scratching or head shaking
- A swollen ear flap
- Balance changes or obvious distress

[Cornell notes](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-topics/how-clean-your-dogs-ears) that infection, allergies, and ear mites can be involved in ear problems. A dark or brown discharge is not something to diagnose from a search result or treat with a home remedy. If the ear is painful, stop handling it and ask your veterinarian what to do next.

## How often should dog ears be cleaned?

There is no safe universal interval for every dog. [VCA says](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/instructions-for-ear-cleaning-in-dogs) that some healthy dogs may never need routine cleaning and that over-cleaning can irritate the canal. Cornell describes maintenance cleaning for dogs prone to ear problems, while also noting that frequency depends on the dog and the reason for cleaning.

Ask your veterinarian or groomer to connect the schedule to your dog’s actual coat, ear shape, swimming, bathing, allergies, and history. After swimming or bathing, ask about drying and whether a gentle check is enough. A calendar copied from another dog is not a medical plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can groomers clean dogs’ ears?

Yes, many groomers offer routine cleaning for healthy ears, either as an add-on or part of a bath or full groom. A groomer should stop and refer you to a veterinarian when the ear is painful, inflamed, or showing signs of a possible medical problem.

### How much do groomers charge for ear cleaning?

Prices depend on the provider and service format. Petco’s current service page lists $12 in most markets, but local prices, bundles, and availability can differ, so confirm the local menu before booking.

### Can I use peroxide on my dog’s ears?

Do not choose hydrogen peroxide or alcohol for an inflamed ear unless your veterinarian specifically recommends it. Cornell and VCA both advise using an appropriate veterinary ear-cleaning solution and stopping if the process causes pain.

### How do you get brown gunk out of a dog’s ear?

Do not assume brown material is ordinary wax. Brown or heavy discharge can occur with an ear problem, so ask a veterinarian before trying to flush or treat the ear, especially if there is odor, redness, itching, or pain.

### Should I clean my dog’s ears before a grooming appointment?

Ask the groomer first. Cleaning immediately before the appointment may be unnecessary, and a painful or abnormal ear should be assessed by a veterinarian rather than handled as routine grooming. Bring any product or schedule instructions your veterinarian has already provided.
