# Style pass rules

Work one section at a time. Keep every number, link, quote and source name.
After each section, read it aloud in your head. If it sounds like a brochure
or a support bot, do it again.

The "before" examples are real sentences from GroomLocal guides (2026-09).

## 1. Put the answer first

Start each post and each section with the answer, not with a setup line.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Whether you're a first-time owner or a seasoned pro, grooming plays a crucial role..." | "Most dogs need a bath every 4 to 6 weeks." |
| "The exact answer depends on the salon, the dog's age and risk, and local requirements, so..." | "Expect to show proof of rabies vaccination. Some salons also want Bordetella. Ask when you book." |

## 2. Break the formulas

| Pattern | Before | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "X is not A; it is B" | "Useful information is not a confession; it is the map." | State B plainly: "Tell the groomer about the snapping and the dryer panic. They can only plan for what they know about." |
| "Not just X, but Y" | "Grooming isn't just about looks, it's about health." | "Regular grooming catches skin problems early." |
| Abstract list of three | "...fewer surprises, less scrambling, and a calmer appointment." | Keep the one item that is concrete and true, or cut the sentence. Concrete lists ("bath, nails and ears") are fine. |
| Recap ending | A "The short version" or "Final thoughts" section | Cut it. End on the last useful step or the call to action. |
| "-ing" tail | "...consistent routine, highlighting the importance of..." | Cut the tail, or make it its own claim with evidence. |
| Transition stack | "Additionally... Furthermore... Moreover..." | Delete the transitions. Start each sentence with its point. |
| Vague source | "Experts say..." / "Groomers recommend..." | Name and link the source, or cut the claim. |
| Hook question | "Have you ever wondered...?" | Cut. Start with the answer. |
| Copula swap | "serves as", "stands as", "acts as" | "is" |

## 3. Fix the voice

- **Use contractions** where you would say them aloud: don't, it's, you'll,
  they're, isn't. Keep "do not" for a safety warning that needs weight.
- **Talk to one reader.** "You" and "your dog". Don't open with a list of
  reader types.
- **Use one hedge for each claim.** Give the common case plainly, then give
  the exception once. "Some salons may often require" is three hedges for one fact.
- **Use plain words.** Each "ai-vocab" line in the scan has a plain swap.
  Don't replace one fancy word with another fancy synonym.
- **State a reasoned opinion only when the author holds it.** "We'd book a
  mobile groomer for a senior dog that hates car rides" is good. An opinion
  that you invent for the author is fabrication.

## 4. Fix the rhythm

Models write sentences of 12 to 25 words, one after another. People don't.

- After two long sentences, write a short one. Six words is enough.
- Let one paragraph in a section be one or two sentences.
- Vary sentence openings. Don't start three sentences in a row with the same word.
- Targets from the scanner: sentence-length CV of 0.45 or more, and at least
  10% short sentences (7 words or fewer). These are guides, not goals. Don't
  chop good sentences to hit a number.

## 5. Use specifics, not adjectives

Replace an adjective with the fact behind it: a number, a time, a place, a
breed, a price, a date. "A long appointment" becomes "2 to 3 hours for a
doodle with a full coat". Every new fact needs a source or real input. The
compare gate flags each new number.

## 6. Formatting

- Match the site's heading case and don't mix cases in one post.
- Keep question headings for the FAQ, and for sections that match a real
  People Also Ask query. Elsewhere, use a plain statement that tells the reader
  what the section gives them.
- Use bold for a few key numbers at most. No emoji. No horizontal rules between
  sections.
- Use a table only for real comparisons (price by size, service by
  frequency). Don't use a table to hold prose.

## 7. Do not

- Don't add typos, slang or random fragments to "look human".
- Don't run text through paraphrasers or "AI humanizer" tools.
- Don't use a thesaurus. Use plain words.
- Don't change the meaning, numbers, sources or links.
- Don't edit to lower an AI-detector score. Edit for the reader.
