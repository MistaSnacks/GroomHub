If your dog has tight mats that will not separate comfortably, arrange a professional assessment. Do not pull through them or cut close to the skin with scissors. A groomer may recommend clipping the coat shorter; painful skin, wounds, swelling, or a dog that cannot be handled safely may require veterinary care first.

## Why Mats Need Attention

Mats are tangled clumps of hair that can become dense and tight against the skin. They may conceal irritation, restrict movement, or make routine cleaning difficult. The [ASPCA's report on severe coat matting](https://www.aspca.org/news/haircut-could-save-life-preventing-your-pets-coat-matting) documents how even apparently minor mats can develop into serious skin problems.

Do not assume that every lump is only fur. If an area is painful or you cannot see the skin beneath it, stop manipulating it and ask for an assessment.

## Check the Coat Gently

Part a small section of hair where your dog is comfortable being touched. A comb should move through the section without dragging skin or requiring force. Stop at resistance rather than trying to push the comb underneath a tight mat.

Common places to check include behind the ears, under collars and harnesses, the armpits, belly, leg feathering, and the rear. Long and curly coats are vulnerable, and double-coated dogs can develop mats and impacted undercoat too.

| What you find | Practical next step |
|---|---|
| A small loose tangle that separates easily and comfortably | Gentle maintenance may be possible; ask for a technique demonstration if unsure |
| Dense knots, repeated resistance, or skin pulling | Stop brushing that area and contact a groomer |
| Extensive matting, odor, pain, wounds, swelling, or discharge | Contact a veterinarian and describe what you can see |
| A dog panicking or attempting to bite during handling | Stop and arrange a safe handling plan with professional help |

These are decision prompts, not a home diagnostic scale.

## What Not to Do at Home

The [ASPCA grooming handout](https://www.aspca.org/sites/default/files/upload/images/pet_grooming_tips_flyer.pdf) specifically warns against scissors near the skin and recommends professional help when unsure. Skin can be pulled into a mat, making it difficult to distinguish hair from tissue.

Do not force a dematting rake through a resistant coat, attempt a close shave on a moving dog, or soak a tightly matted coat to make the problem disappear. [VCA's coat-care guidance](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/grooming-and-coat-care-for-your-dog) advises addressing tangles before bathing because washing can make them harder to remove.

If the coat is contaminated or soiled, explain the situation when you call for help so the provider can advise on the next step. Embarrassment should not delay necessary care.

## What Happens at the Groomer?

### 1. Coat and Handling Assessment

The groomer checks where the mats sit, how much skin can be examined, and what handling your dog tolerates. They may recommend a veterinarian instead of proceeding at the salon.

### 2. Agree on the Service and Cost

Ask which areas can be maintained and which may need clipping. Confirm the expected length, any additional time charge, and whether the groomer will call if the plan changes. Request a realistic finish rather than insisting that all length be saved.

### 3. Remove Mats With an Appropriate Method

Loose tangles and tight pelting need different approaches. Close clipping can be the kinder option when brushing would pull painfully, but it is skilled work and is not guaranteed to be painless or risk-free. The professional should select equipment and technique based on the coat and skin; an online guide should not prescribe a blade number.

### 4. Discuss Aftercare

Ask what skin changes were found, which services were completed, and when to resume brushing. Seek veterinary advice for persistent discomfort, wounds, swelling, or unusual licking. Regrowth varies by coat and health, so do not rely on a promise that the coat will return to its previous length in two or three months.

## How Much Does Dematting Cost?

There is no reliable flat price for severe matting. The quote may combine a base groom, extra handling time, coat removal, and any separate veterinary care. A short coat after the appointment does not mean it was quick or easy to achieve.

Ask whether extra coat work is charged by time, by area, or as a revised service price. Agree on a contact point before additional charges are incurred. Our [regional grooming price guide](/blog/dog-grooming-cost-seattle-portland-2026) provides linked salon examples; those routine menus are not quotes for a severely matted dog.

If cost is a barrier, say so when requesting help. Ask about the least extensive safe service and any local assistance the provider can actually verify. Do not assume that a “comfort groom” has a standard price or that frequent visits always cost less over a year.

## Prevent Mats From Returning

Ask the groomer to show you one section of your dog's actual coat: how to part it, which brush fits it, how lightly to work, and how to check the result with a comb. Surface brushing alone can miss deeper tangles.

Choose a length you can maintain. If long legs or a fluffy face repeatedly tangle, discuss simplifying those areas. Keep home sessions short and comfortable; increasing brushing force is not a substitute for a manageable haircut.

After wet outings, remove loose debris and dry carefully without vigorous rubbing. Do not rush a painful brush-out to satisfy a rule about brushing wet hair. For practical cleanup, see our [rain and mud guide](/blog/rain-mud-fur-pnw-grooming-survival-guide).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I brush out a severely matted dog?

Do not try to force through tight or extensive mats. Arrange an assessment; preserving hair length is not worth painful handling or skin injury.

### Will the groomer have to shave my dog?

Possibly. The answer depends on how tightly the coat is matted, skin condition, and safe handling. Ask the groomer to explain the proposed length and any limits before starting.

### Can double-coated dogs get matted?

Yes. Long hair and dense undercoat can tangle or compact. A rule against routine cosmetic shaving should not prevent a veterinarian or groomer from addressing a painful coat appropriately.

### When should I call a vet instead of a groomer?

Call for pain, wounds, swelling, discharge, extensive skin involvement, or handling that cannot be done safely. Tell the clinic if mats cover the affected area so they can plan the examination.
